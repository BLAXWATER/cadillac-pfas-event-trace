## Supported findings with exact PDF-page anchors

1. **Groundwater framework is heterogeneous and clay barriers are discontinuous.** The report describes alternating sand/fine gravel and lacustrine clays, shallow clay less than 10 to about 22 feet and absent northeast, intermittent intermediate clay, and possible deep leakage/alternative paths (PDF 21–22). Appendix A repeats that the shallow and intermediate clays are intermittent/discontinuous and that shallow/intermediate units merge northeast (PDF 109–110). Appendix C describes 0–2 feet of shallow clay in a transition area that may not be contiguous (PDF 135). This is historical hydrogeologic context, not proof of a present PFAS pathway.

2. **Historical hydraulic capture conclusions are conditional.** The executive summary says the combined systems capture the VOC plume and that Cr(VI) cleanup *may* be achieved (PDF 9). The body repeatedly qualifies capture using sparse levels, analytical assumptions, partial penetration and model particle tracking (PDF 33–36, 114–116, 125–128, 134–138). It does not apportion sources between Rexair and OU2 (PDF 36) and does not resolve the CW7/subdivision source (PDF 46).

3. **Possible vertical leakage is modeled/estimated, not a direct current-flow measurement.** The report gives Keck's historical estimate of about 100 gpm and MDEQ-model values of about 210 gpm pre-remediation, 130 gpm in 2004 and 115 gpm in 2006 (PDF 46, 114). These values support a possible mechanism but are not modern PFAS transport measurements.

4. **Historical chemistry remained complex despite many downward trends.** The October 4, 2006 PAM analysis found multiple VOC UCL standard exceedances while many series trended downward (PDF 162–186; individual plots PDF 187–598). Downward is therefore not synonymous with clean, compliant or closed.

5. **Recommendations are not completed actions.** The report recommends four Cr(VI) sampling rounds, sustained O&M, model optimization, geophysics, source characterization, dividing-streamline work and integrated monitoring/database improvements (PDF 47–49, 105). The supplied document does not prove those recommendations were completed.

## Publication-safe characterization

This is an April 2007 historical hydraulic-capture evaluation for the Kysor-Northernaire Superfund setting. It documents complex sand aquifers, intermittent/discontinuous clay, historical groundwater gradients, remediation pumping and statistical trends for VOCs/chromium. It predates the PFAS investigation and **does not establish PFAS source attribution or a current Wexford-to-Plett migration pathway**. Any catalog block should be placed under Groundwater & Hydrogeology, label capture results as modeled/historical, cite the exact PDF pages above and disclose the missing-map-layer defects.
